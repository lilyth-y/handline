⭐ Head Line

Look at that line crossing your palm. That is the path your thoughts travel: the head line.

What kind of line is it?

The head line starts between the thumb and index finger, near where the life line begins, either sharing the start or very close to it, and runs across the palm toward the pinky side. In some hands it travels with the life line for a while before parting; in others it starts apart and goes its own way.

In traditional palmistry the head line is not a ruler for how smart you are. It reads how you spread out your thoughts, how fast you reach a decision, and whether you tend to see reality or possibility. Hoa Cáo Tiên does not accept that a short line means a lacking mind. Reading a short line as "gets to the point and decides quickly" and a long one as "thinks it through to the end" is far kinder and far more sensible.

In traditional palmistry

A head line running straight and level was read as a sense of reality and practical judgment, one sloping gently downward as imagination and intuition, and one that undulates like a wave as wide interests and flexible thinking. An old tale says that a line dipping too deep may linger in daydreams, but that too is a hint about temperament, not a verdict.

Starting joined to the life line was read as careful and well prepared, and starting apart as independent and driven. A break meant a turning point in thinking or a shift of interests, and branches meant thoughts blooming in several directions. A crisp, clear line can mean a time of good focus, and one that looks chained can mean a time of holding many things at once.

The head line is also discussed through its distance from the heart line. A wide gap is read as a generous heart, a narrow one as sensitivity or a tendency to tension. The gap is not an absolute personality score either.

How the Hoa Cáo Tiên app looks at it

In a flat photo, the app measures the head line's relative length, how much it curves and slopes, and where it breaks or branches. It does not look at the 3D shape of your hand and only uses ratios based on palm width.

Whether the line starts together with the life line, and how straight or curved it is, are used when talking about how you start things and the grain of your thinking. Light, angle, and how much you spread or curl your fingers can change how the curve looks, so it helps to pick the photo where your palm is flattest and the lines are clearest.

The result does not predict intelligence or school grades. It is a light palm reading that sets the grain of your palm beside traditional stories.
